STEP-BY-STEP MODULES

Detailed Curriculum & Labs

Module 01 Motion Principles & After Effects Essentials 4 Lessons ⌄
Module Focus: Master the After Effects interface and the core principles of animation timing.
  • Workspace navigation and project structure
  • Keyframing and spatial interpolation
  • The 12 principles of animation applied to UI
  • Rendering fundamentals
Key Concepts: CompositionsGraph EditorTrack MattesParenting
1.1 Navigating After Effects & Project Setup
Taught: Interface layout, compositions, frame rates, and importing layered assets.
Learner Lab: Creates a structured project folder and sets up a standard 1080p composition.
Why It Matters: Proper setup prevents frame rate mismatches and chaotic, unmanageable projects later.
Expected Deliverable: An organized project ready for animation.
1.2 The Art of Keyframing & Easing
Taught: Linear vs Bezier keyframes, and using the Graph Editor for speed control.
Learner Lab: Animates a bouncing ball and UI elements using speed graph adjustments.
Why It Matters: Easing is what separates amateur, robotic animation from professional, organic motion.
Expected Deliverable: Smooth, realistic object motion with weight and anticipation.
1.3 Working with Masks & Track Mattes
Taught: Creating alpha mattes, luma mattes, and animating mask paths.
Learner Lab: Builds a clean text reveal utilizing track mattes.
Why It Matters: Essential for seamless transitions, text reveals, and complex compositing.
Expected Deliverable: A professional lower-third graphic ready for broadcast.
1.4 Pre-composing & Null Objects
Taught: Grouping layers with pre-comps and parenting to master null objects.
Learner Lab: Rigs a mechanical arm using parenting chains.
Why It Matters: Keeps timelines clean and allows for complex hierarchical animations without breaking keyframes.
Expected Deliverable: A complex animation driven by a single master control.
🛠️ Hands-on Lab: Animate a multi-layered logo reveal.
📝 Practical Assignment: Deliverable: 5-second logo animation with easing and mattes.
💼 Commercial Application: Logo intros for corporate videos and YouTube channels.
🎯 Expected Outcome: Solid grasp of 2D animation fundamentals in AE.
Module 02 Shape Animation & Kinetic Typography 4 Lessons ⌄
Module Focus: Create dynamic vector animations and automate motion with expressions and text animators.
  • Advanced shape layer modifiers
  • Text animator range selectors
  • Wiggle and loop expressions
  • Kinetic typography layouts
Key Concepts: Trim PathsRange SelectorsExpressionsAudio Sync
2.1 Advanced Shape Layer Modifiers
Taught: Trim paths, repeater, wiggle paths, and merge paths within shape layers.
Learner Lab: Creates bursting fireworks and radial UI elements.
Why It Matters: Allows for complex, procedural vector animations without keyframing every single element.
Expected Deliverable: A complex abstract vector background.
2.2 Mastering Text Animators
Taught: Range selectors, animating by word/line/character, and per-character 3D.
Learner Lab: Builds 3 unique, reusable text animation presets.
Why It Matters: Text animators are incredibly powerful for automated typographic motion without manual keyframing.
Expected Deliverable: Dynamic text reveals saved as animation presets.
2.3 Kinetic Typography Workflows
Taught: Syncing text to audio beats, dynamic camera moves, and layout transitions.
Learner Lab: Animates a 15-second voiceover quote.
Why It Matters: Highly demanded for commercials, social media ads, and lyric videos.
Expected Deliverable: A punchy, well-timed kinetic typography sequence.
2.4 Essential Expressions for Motion
Taught: Writing basic JavaScript for AE: time, wiggle, loopOut, and linking properties via pickwhip.
Learner Lab: Rigs a looping background element and a wiggling camera.
Why It Matters: Saves hours of keyframing and allows for procedural, scalable animation.
Expected Deliverable: An automated, code-driven animation sequence.
🛠️ Hands-on Lab: Build a kinetic typography scene synced to an audio clip.
📝 Practical Assignment: Deliverable: 15-second kinetic typography quote.
💼 Commercial Application: Social media video ads and lyric videos.
🎯 Expected Outcome: Ability to create engaging text and vector motion efficiently.
Module 03 3D Integration & Cinema 4D Lite 4 Lessons ⌄
Module Focus: Bridge the gap between 2D compositing and true 3D using After Effects and C4D Lite.
  • 2.5D camera techniques in AE
  • Cineware workflow
  • Basic 3D modeling and lighting
  • MoGraph effectors
Key Concepts: Z-SpaceCinewareMoGraphMulti-pass Compositing
3.1 Working in 2.5D Space
Taught: Converting layers to 3D, Z-depth setup, multi-node cameras, and depth of field.
Learner Lab: Creates a fly-through animation across multiple 3D text layers.
Why It Matters: Adds cinematic depth and production value to flat vector designs.
Expected Deliverable: A spatial composition with depth and a smooth camera move.
3.2 Intro to Cinema 4D Lite & Cineware
Taught: Interface basics, creating C4D files directly in AE, and the live Cineware bridge.
Learner Lab: Creates a basic 3D scene and links it live to an After Effects composition.
Why It Matters: Enables seamless integration of true 3D assets without tedious render exports.
Expected Deliverable: A live 3D asset running and updating within AE.
3.3 Modeling & Materials Basics in C4D
Taught: Primitives, splines, extrude NURBS, and basic PBR materials.
Learner Lab: Extrudes a 2D vector logo into 3D and applies glossy/metallic materials.
Why It Matters: Allows creation of 3D logos, text, and simple objects for client branding.
Expected Deliverable: A photorealistic 3D logo asset.
3.4 MoGraph Effectors & AE Compositing
Taught: Using Cloners and Random effectors in C4D, then extracting 3D scene data into AE for multi-pass compositing.
Learner Lab: Animates a grid of objects forming a logo, then composites it with 2D overlays in AE.
Why It Matters: MoGraph is the core of C4D's power for procedural motion graphics.
Expected Deliverable: A highly polished 2D/3D hybrid logo reveal.
🛠️ Hands-on Lab: Create a 3D product shot or logo reveal.
📝 Practical Assignment: Deliverable: 3D logo animation composited with 2D elements.
💼 Commercial Application: Premium tech product videos and sports broadcast packages.
🎯 Expected Outcome: Proficiency in the AE to C4D workflow.
Module 04 Character Rigging & Animation 4 Lessons ⌄
Module Focus: Prepare and animate 2D characters for explainer videos and digital campaigns.
  • Illustrator to AE preparation
  • Inverse Kinematics (IK)
  • Walk cycles
  • Facial rigging and lip sync
Key Concepts: Inverse KinematicsWalk CyclePhonemesAnchor Points
4.1 Character Prep & Overlord Workflow
Taught: Structuring Illustrator files, anchor point placement, and grouping for limbs.
Learner Lab: Organizes a character file in Illustrator and imports it correctly into AE.
Why It Matters: Improper file prep makes rigging impossible and breaks character joints.
Expected Deliverable: A ready-to-rig character composition.
4.2 Rigging with IK (Inverse Kinematics)
Taught: Setting up limbs using basic expressions or free scripts like Duik Bassel.
Learner Lab: Rigs a bipedal character's arms and legs.
Why It Matters: IK makes animating arms and legs drastically faster and more natural than FK.
Expected Deliverable: A fully functional, controllable character rig.
4.3 The Walk Cycle
Taught: Contact, down, pass, and up poses; creating seamless looping walk cycles.
Learner Lab: Animates a seamless looping walk cycle.
Why It Matters: The fundamental test of character animation skills.
Expected Deliverable: A character walking convincingly in place.
4.4 Facial Rigging & Lip Sync
Taught: Joystick controllers for head turns and time-remapping for phoneme mouth shapes.
Learner Lab: Rigs a face and lip-syncs a short audio clip.
Why It Matters: Adds personality, emotion, and narrative capability to characters.
Expected Deliverable: A speaking, expressive character animation.
🛠️ Hands-on Lab: Rig and animate a character performing a specific action (e.g., waving, walking).
📝 Practical Assignment: Deliverable: Character walk cycle + wave animation.
💼 Commercial Application: Start-up explainer videos and educational content.
🎯 Expected Outcome: Ability to rig and animate a 2D character from scratch.
Module 05 Broadcast Design Capstone & Showreel 4 Lessons ⌄
Module Focus: Compile, polish, and present a professional motion design showreel to secure employment or freelance clients.
  • Broadcast identity design
  • Showreel pacing and editing
  • Sound design for motion graphics
  • Portfolio presentation
Key Concepts: Broadcast PackageShowreel EditingSound DesignPortfolio Presentation
5.1 Designing a Broadcast Package
Taught: Creating a cohesive set of graphics (bumpers, lower thirds, transitions) based on a network brief.
Learner Lab: Designs and animates a 3-part broadcast graphics package.
Why It Matters: Proves the ability to maintain brand consistency across multiple dynamic assets.
Expected Deliverable: A cohesive capstone project demonstrating advanced design skills.
5.2 Editing the Showreel in Premiere Pro
Taught: Culling weak projects, cutting to the beat, intro/outro design, and pacing.
Learner Lab: Edits their best course projects into a tight 60-second sequence.
Why It Matters: A reel is only as strong as its weakest shot; editing dictates the perceived energy of your work.
Expected Deliverable: A dynamic, beat-synced showreel draft.
5.3 Sound Design & Final Mix
Taught: Adding swooshes, impacts, risers, and EQing music in Premiere/Audition.
Learner Lab: Adds a comprehensive sound design pass to their showreel.
Why It Matters: Sound design accounts for 50% of the perceived quality and impact of motion graphics.
Expected Deliverable: A punchy, professional-sounding reel.
5.4 Portfolio Launch & Client Pitching
Taught: Building Behance case studies, exporting optimal formats, and setting freelance rates.
Learner Lab: Publishes their reel and finalizes their online portfolio.
Why It Matters: Technical skills must be paired with business acumen to succeed in the industry.
Expected Deliverable: A public-facing, professional motion designer presence ready for clients.
🛠️ Hands-on Lab: Edit, mix, and finalize the 60-second capstone showreel.
📝 Practical Assignment: Deliverable: Final 60-second Motion Graphics Showreel.
💼 Commercial Application: Applying for agency roles or pitching freelance clients.
🎯 Expected Outcome: A fully finished, industry-ready portfolio and showreel.
