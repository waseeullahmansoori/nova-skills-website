1.1 Motion Graphics Fundamentals
Taught: Distinction between motion design, live-action video editing, and visual effects (VFX). Frame rates (24fps cinematic, 30fps broadcast, 60fps high-motion), standard delivery resolutions (1080p Full HD, 4K UHD, 9:16 vertical), composition duration, square versus non-square pixel aspect ratios, composition settings setup, and foundational animation planning.
  • Motion design vs video editing: spatial graphic transformation vs sequential storytelling
  • Motion graphics vs VFX: graphic communication vs photorealistic plate modification
  • Frame rates and temporal resolution: 24fps vs 30fps vs 60fps motion smoothness
  • Resolution and pixel aspect ratios: square pixels vs anamorphic distortion
  • Composition settings: dimension presets, start timecode, and background color
  • Animation planning: creative briefs, story frames, and timeline mapping
Learner Lab: Creates properly configured compositions for landscape broadcast and vertical social formats.
Why It Matters: Incorrect composition settings lead to blurry exports, dropped frames, and distorted graphics.
Expected Deliverable: Accurate understanding of composition parameters and digital motion foundations.

  • Searching and applying effects from the Effects & Presets library
  • Preview panel controls: frame skipping, resolution throttling, and RAM caching

  • Overshoot and Follow-through: momentum carrying objects past their resting point

  • Visual rhythm: matching animation cadence to narrative beat and viewer focus

2.1 Keyframe Interpolation
Taught: In-depth breakdown of keyframe interpolation types: Linear, Bezier, Continuous Bezier, Auto Bezier, and Hold keyframes. Understanding the difference between temporal interpolation (speed across time) and spatial interpolation (path through space).
  • Temporal vs spatial interpolation: time velocity vs spatial trajectory
  • Linear keyframes: mechanical, unaccelerated motion and when to avoid it
  • Bezier and Continuous Bezier keyframes for organic speed curves
  • Hold keyframes: instant step-transitions for typography and stepped animation
  • Keyframe Interpolation dialog: setting specific in/out tolerances
  • Fixing spatial boomeranging: removing unintentional spatial Bezier drift
Learner Lab: Inspects and converts keyframes across temporal and spatial dimensions to eliminate unwanted drift.
Why It Matters: Mastering interpolation types prevents erratic curves and delivers intentional motion feel.
Expected Deliverable: Complete command of temporal and spatial keyframe interpolation types.
2.2 Easing
Taught: Principles of natural acceleration and deceleration: Easy Ease, Ease In, Ease Out, keyframe velocity dialog, percentage influence, peak speed control, and crafting dynamic acceleration ramps.
  • The physics of easing: inertia, friction, and natural momentum
  • Easy Ease (F9), Ease In (Shift+F9), and Ease Out (Ctrl+Shift+F9 / Cmd+Shift+F9)
  • Opening the Keyframe Velocity dialog: incoming and outgoing speed values
  • Keyframe influence percentages: default 33.33% vs extreme 75–85% snaps
  • Crafting fast-in slow-out commercial transitions
  • Matching easing curves across multiple properties for unified visual velocity
Learner Lab: Configures keyframe velocity values to create high-impact, snap-easing motion graphics.
Why It Matters: Standard default easing looks generic; custom velocity creates modern, punchy animation.
Expected Deliverable: Ability to configure precise velocity and influence percentages for expressive easing.
2.3 Graph Editor

  • Switching between the Speed Graph and the Value Graph
  • Reading the Speed Graph: height equals velocity in pixels per second
  • Reading the Value Graph: slope equals rate of change over time
  • Manipulating Bezier handles to sculpt snappy acceleration ramps
  • Separating Dimensions (Separate X and Y) for independent value curves
  • Troubleshooting bumpy curves, unintentional speed dips, and sudden plateaus
Learner Lab: Sculpts custom Speed and Value graph curves to transform a robotic move into a silky, organic motion.
Why It Matters: The Graph Editor is the single tool that separates professional motion designers from beginners.
Expected Deliverable: Fluent control of Speed and Value graphs for bespoke curve refinement.

  • Editing Bezier path handles directly in the viewer using the Pen tool
  • Using the Convert Vertex tool to toggle between sharp corners and smooth arcs
  • Auto-Orient: making layers automatically rotate to face along their trajectory

  • Follow-through: allowing appendages and secondary elements to settle late
