4.1 Lighting Fundamentals
Taught: Physics and aesthetics of light: light direction, intensity (Watts/Lumens), softness vs hardness based on light source size, shadow falloff, inverse square law, contrast ratios, and reflection highlights for defining product forms.
  • Light direction and visual form sculpting
  • Light size vs shadow softness (large=soft, small=hard)
  • Inverse square law and light falloff
  • Specular reflections as surface definition
  • Contrast ratios and tonal range
  • Visual readability of product silhouettes
Learner Lab: Sets up a single light source and adjusts size, power, and angle to observe how shadow softness sculpts surface curvature.
Why It Matters: Lighting is what turns flat 3D geometry into a tangible, emotionally compelling product photograph.
Expected Deliverable: Deep comprehension of light physics and its role in revealing 3D forms.
4.2 Blender Light Types
Taught: The four Blender light types: Point light (omnidirectional), Spot light (focused beam with blend/size), Area light (rectangular, square, disk, ellipse softboxes), Sun light (infinite parallel rays); configuring power, color, and size.
  • Point light characteristics and use cases
  • Spot light beam angle and blend feathering
  • Area light shapes (Rectangle, Disk, Ellipse) as softboxes
  • Sun light angle and parallel ray mechanics
  • Power (Watts), Color temperature, and custom hues
  • Custom light falloff and shadow toggles
Learner Lab: Configures rectangular Area lights to simulate photographic softbox diffusers with gradient falloffs.
Why It Matters: Area lights are the primary tool for commercial studio product lighting due to their softbox reflection highlights.
Expected Deliverable: Skill in selecting and configuring the appropriate light type for any lighting scenario.
4.3 Three-Point Studio Lighting
Taught: Classic three-point lighting for products: Key light (primary shape descriptor), Fill light (shadow detail recovery), Rim / Back light (subject separation from backdrop), establishing lighting ratios (e.g. 3:1, 4:1), and bounce light.
  • Key light placement and primary highlight angle
  • Fill light positioning and shadow level control
  • Rim / Back light for edge separation and rim highlights
  • Commercial lighting ratios and contrast management
  • Background isolation and subject separation
  • Preventing washed-out, flat lighting
Learner Lab: Builds a classic 3-point studio lighting rig for a consumer product, dialing in ratios for clean commercial separation.
Why It Matters: Three-point lighting is the foundational industry standard for commercial product photography and visualization.
Expected Deliverable: Mastery of three-point lighting setups and contrast ratio control.
4.4 HDRI Environment Lighting
Taught: Image-Based Lighting (IBL) using High Dynamic Range Images (HDRI): World shader setup, Environment Texture node, HDRI rotation via Mapping node, balancing HDRI ambient light with direct Area lights, and licensing.
  • What makes an HDRI image high dynamic range
  • World shader setup with Environment Texture
  • Rotating and scaling HDRIs with Texture Coordinate & Mapping
  • Using studio HDRIs vs outdoor environment HDRIs
  • Combining HDRI ambient fill with targeted Area key lights
  • Asset sourcing and commercial licensing rules
Learner Lab: Sets up a studio HDRI environment, rotates the lighting to align specular highlights, and adds a softbox key light.
Why It Matters: HDRIs provide complex, natural environmental reflections that single digital lights cannot replicate.
Expected Deliverable: Ability to integrate HDRI lighting seamlessly with targeted studio lamps.
4.5 Product Lighting Scenarios
