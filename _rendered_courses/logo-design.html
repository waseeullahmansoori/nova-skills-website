Prior experience is helpful but not strictly required. The curriculum introduces vector paths, anchor point editing, and the Pen Tool systematically in Module 3. What is essential is basic computer literacy and a willingness to sketch and iterate ideas on paper.

Yes. Module 1 is dedicated to research and discovery: unpacking client briefs, identifying brand personality attributes, analyzing competitor visual clichés, exploring visual metaphors, and establishing criteria for distinctiveness.

Yes. Module 2 emphasizes thumbnail sketching and rapid ideation. You will learn how to visually brainstorm with mind maps, explore negative space, experiment with letterforms, and evaluate concept silhouettes before opening Adobe Illustrator.

Yes. You will study figure-ground relationships and negative-space construction techniques using Illustrator's Shape Builder and Pathfinder tools, learning to craft purposeful visual double-meanings without forcing artificial visual tricks.

Yes. Module 3 provides deep technical instruction on Bézier handles, direction lines, anchor point reduction, corner vs. smooth points, path simplification, and creating pristine, mathematically clean vector paths.

Yes. You will learn wordmark craftsmanship, including font pairing, letterform modification, tracking, custom kerning, ligature concepts, baseline alignment, and preserving live editable text masters alongside outlined release copies.

Yes. You will explore geometric grids, circle construction, proportional ratios, and symmetry. However, the course teaches that geometric grids are construction aids, not rigid formulas—optical balance always takes precedence over mathematical alignment.

Optical refinement is adjusting vector shapes to compensate for human visual perception. It includes applying overshoot to circular forms so they appear equal in height to flat letters, adjusting letter spacing optically, and centering irregular symbols visually rather than mathematically.

Every student creates three distinct original concept directions supported by research, mind maps, and sketches during Module 2, exploring varied symbolic and typographic approaches.

From the three initial concepts, you will select and fully develop two complete vector logo solutions in Module 3, taking both through precision vector construction, typography, optical tuning, and contextual testing.

Yes. You will test and build 100% black (monochrome) and white reversed-out versions for both refined solutions, ensuring the mark's silhouette remains powerful and legible without relying on color or gradients.

Yes. Module 4 includes stress testing at microscopic scales (16×16px favicon, 32×32px app icon, 64×64px avatar, mobile headers), teaching you how to create simplified compact variants when detailed marks degrade at small sizes.

Yes. You work with native, layered Adobe Illustrator (.ai) files and learn industry standards for organizing artwork layers, naming conventions, eliminating stray points, and packaging editable source files for clients.

Yes. Module 5 covers multi-format export standards: clean scalable vector graphics (SVG) for web, vector PDFs for print, and transparent PNGs at multiple resolutions for digital applications.
